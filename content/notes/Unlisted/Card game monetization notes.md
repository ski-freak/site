Notes for video on how the business models of card games impact the experience of playing those games, and how we hope to avoid some common problems.

- Intro to the current state of card games, what games we are actually talking about (player vs player card games that release new card sets regularly, so you kind of stick with the game long term whether it's for the competitive aspect, the ongoing worldbuilding/narrative, playing with friends, deckbuilding, or whatever else)
- We were kind of priced out of paper magic, paper cards costing an insane amount of money in many cases. The huge price tag on it's own is horrible even before getting into other stuff
- Access to the game pieces being the issue, not so much the fact that expensive collectibles exist sometimes (they just must be cosmetic things, ideally you could have them as tournament prizes for example).

# Direct negative effects on the player:
- The pitfalls of card packs as monetization for online card games
  - Quests & grinding - being pushed to grind solo modes or menial quests that aren't actually enjoyable just so you can get to the part of the game that you actually want to play.
  - Hearthstone dust system in particular being an emotionally really bad experience
- Being stuck playing one or a few decks and not much variety in what you can play, just picking one meta deck to be playing and thats it. Not being able to experience the full scope of the game.
- Generally feeling bad when you invest into some deck and then you can't play it anymore.
  - Having to be more hesitant to make balance changes or bans due to it costing people money directly.
- Not being able to deckbuild freely

### Addressing a couple common defenses of the status quo:
- No, people having access to cards does not lead everyone to all play the same deck, that's stupid and not how it plays out in practice as evidenced by Penny Dreadful. If anything, limiting access to cards means that players are stuck playing only one or a few decks, at which point they are pressured to pick a meta deck because otherwise they run a high risk of always losing and not having fun. Makes it harder to experiment with things. 
    - Also even if it were true that players having equal access to cards means that the meta will be less diverse, you would then have to essentially make the argument that we should screw over all of the players who don't have a lot of money so that the players who do have a lot of money to spend can play against a slightly greater variety of decks. Which is... super weird, and bad.
- Some people like the collectability aspect, and think removing it takes away half of what makes card games engaging. Enjoying collecting is fine, and we still want to have some stuff for people to work for and collect, likely focusing somewhat on things you collect by earning them rather than buying them or lucking into them through packs, although that may also exist as a way to allow people to essentially donate to the game they enjoy playing. Having spending money collecting cards as a barrier to enjoying the actual gameplay is unacceptable to us and simply doesn't align with our priorities, which is to first and foremost make a fun game that everyone can play and hopefully some people will love. If you want a game that is all about collecting cards from card packs or expensive products / secondary markets, there are already a ton of those that exist, we hope to fill the niche for people who care more about the actual gameplay and barrier to entry to it, which personally I think might be a large number of people and if anything the real niche is people with money to burn on cards.

# Negative effects on the business incentives & overall trajectory of the game:

- Requirement number 1 for a healthy paper model: The average price of cards to be low, and comparable to buying regular board/card games. This is the obvious one, and if it is not the case, you will be pricing players out of your game which is bad.

- It being in the card company's best interest for there to be cards that cost a large amount of money to sell more card packs.
  - Reprints being the common solution to card prices long term, but this is a kind of manual solution. There are constantly cards becoming more costly, and it's a game of whack a mole to try to keep reprinting them. Inevitably because it is not in the company's best interest financially to be good at this game of whack a mole, they won't be, and there will be a constant crisis of cards costing too much and there always needing to be more reprints than there are. It's also just hard to fit reprints into the actual sets you are designing, especially when you need to design them in advance.
    - This points to requirement number 2 for a healthy paper model: There must be a limit on how much it can cost to acquire a given card as a player, and this limit must be naturally enforced by the business model and not something that must be actively worked at to keep cards under. 


- Living card game model has not worked, and this sort of points at what needs to happen for it to work for players. With lcg they only released full sets, and so that's great when the game is just one or two sets, but as it expands, it becomes a huge entry cost since you can't really just pick up singles or individual decks as easily.
  - Requirement number 3 for a healthy paper model: We need some kind of way for players to get just the cards they need to build the deck they want to build/play.

# Solutions:

We'll be going over online and in paper, keep in mind that we aren't really particularly close to publishing anything in either medium, but when we eventually get to these bridges, this is what we are what we are currently planning on doing in order to avoid becoming the thing we originally hated.

## Online
  - Online you don't need to contend with scarcity in the first place so it's relatively simple to fix.
  - Simple yearly DLC / Subscription model to just have all the cards in the game.
  - Probably also have some other random cosmetic bits you can


## Paper

Paper is kind of a long ways off and it's uncertain if we'll ever get to a point where it's relevant, as our initial plan for paper release is essentially to provide the initial set of cards as a standalone card game you can get and play just like a board game, but we have done a bit of theorizing on how, if we end up releasing cards regularly in paper, we might keep the prices of cards under control.

Also worth noting that distribution via booster packs is a notoriously expensive and involved process, and very few companies are able to successfully do it, especially long term.
The paper requirements from before and how we can address them:
 1. We need the average price of cards to be low, and comparable to buying regular board/card games.
     - Selling full sets of cards without players needing to go through booster packs if they don't want to should handle this.
 - 2. We need some kind of way for players to get just the cards they need to build the deck they want to build/play.
     -  To some extent this is something that is determined by how many people are playing the game in a local area, and how often they end up with random cards they don't need or are willing to trade away. With only full sets, people tend to want to keep their full set of cards. Booster packs create a situation where players end up with extra copies or random cards they don't necessarily want, and overall create an environment where even if you have a full set of some category of cards, you are likely willing to part with the ones you don't actually see yourself using. Right now I think what I expect to be the best bet for achieving a similar effect without the difficulty of booster packs is to have a bit of variety to the products you can buy to have cards to play with, which will inevitably end up with some overlap in the cards within them. Beyond full sets, you could have for example pre constructed decks for people to play with, things made to be drafted, or collections of cards from full sets (this is something that could be given out similar to how packs are as prizes for local game store events). My hope or prediction is that while this would start off as the simple board game model where everyone just buys a set of cards and has them, over time as more cards are released and more people end up with random cards they might not want or more copies of cards than they need, the environment will become one where players trading cards with themselves or an lgs is more common. In the absence of that, having pre constructed decks available will at least mean that new players have an easy way to jump into constructed without needing to buy a bunch of different full sets to get the cards they need.
 - 3.  There must be a limit on how much it can cost to get a given card (by this i mean the cheapest version of it), to avoid needing to constantly fight back against higher demand or lower supply cards costing large amounts of money.
     - For this the obvious solution is to sell singles, possibly print to order. Even if you sell them at a price that makes it so people normally never want to buy them or they may even be cheaper on a secondary market if that exists, giving people the option to buy any card for a fixed price ensures that if a card is in such high demand or low supply, it will always be available to players without them needing to hand over an arm and a leg. It's impossible to give an exact number for what would make the most sense as a price right now, but if I had to take a wild guess I'd say something in the ballpark of four bucks. Something high enough that under normal circumstances people generally buy the actual products or get cards from their local game store, but beyond that as low as possible.
